A timeline that survives a phone

Twenty-four hours across 340 pt of phone width gives each hour 14 points, so a ten-minute session renders 2.4 points wide — thinner than the line under this sentence. No font fixes that. Below: the same real data, drawn both ways, at three widths. Turn the axis to Horizontal at phone width to watch it fail.

Decision 01

Spend the axis you have in surplus

Scrolling down is free and native on a phone; horizontal width is fixed and scarce. So time — which needs unbounded room — goes on the free axis, and the app name goes on the scarce one, where text reads anyway.

At 64 px/hour that same ten-minute session is 11 × 250 instead of 340 × 2.4. Room for a name, a duration, and the shading.

Decision 02

Devices are a gutter, not equal columns

R11 says the raw per-device tracks are always available underneath. But they are reference, not the thing you read. Three equal columns on a 412 pt phone gives everything 130 pt and ruins all three.

Narrow: a 10 pt stripe per device says who was awake here at a glance. Wide: the stripes become full labelled columns. Same data, same component.

Decision 03

Collapse the dead time, keep the glance

Most of a tracked day is nothing. Uncollapsed, you flick past sleep for six screens. Quiet runs over 25 minutes become one 2h 14m quiet divider — the day drops from ~20 screens to about 3.

Collapsing costs you the whole day at once, which horizontal gives free. The minimap strip buys it back: full 24 hours, always visible, showing density and where you are.

Rejected

The chronological card list

Sessions in order, no time scale. Reads beautifully on a phone and is a fraction of the work.

It kills the feature. Contention shading is a claim about area — these two devices were both busy for this much of the same hour. Drop proportional time and R8 has nothing left to say. That settles it: proportionality is non-negotiable here.

Integration

One component, orientation as a prop

Upstream will not accept a second timeline page, and they'd be right. But vertical and horizontal are not two pages — they are one mapping from time to distance, with the axis as a variable. Data, colours, hit-testing and shading are identical.

So the PR is “the timeline gains a vertical mode below ~700 px”. Desktop unchanged, no migration, additive. The multi-device combined view stays in our fork, because upstream has nothing to merge it into.

Honest cost

vis-timeline does not fully go away

vis-timeline is imported in seven files — Timeline, Bucket, Report, the two selectable-view components, the label helper, and its own wrapper. Replacing all of it is a bigger project than this one.

So two renderers coexist for a while: ours for the combined view and for Timeline at narrow width, vis-timeline for everything else. That duplication sits behind one component boundary rather than in a duplicated page — and it shrinks over time instead of growing.

Example data, not your real day. Two devices, hand-built to the shape of the 2026-09-09 verified run so the proportions and the contention are realistic. Durations, totals and the shaded overlaps below are computed live from that data, not drawn by hand — the combined total is a true union measure, never the sum of the devices (R6).
